The most avoidable mistake is arranging removal before deciding what outcome you need. A collection, a donation and a disposal appointment have different purposes. Another is leaving furniture outdoors without permission or instructions: weather can damage an item that might otherwise remain useful.

## Plan the details

Do not rely on a neighbour’s recollection of municipal rules. Check the current instructions for your own locality, especially for unusual items, opening times and access conditions. Keep reusable goods dry, describe damage and avoid mixing dangerous or unknown materials into ordinary household boxes. Rehomeo does not publish municipality-specific disposal rules.

## A practical example

An otherwise useful chair left in rain while waiting for collection may become harder to reuse. Keep it indoors or in appropriate protected storage until the agreed time. If local disposal is your chosen route, follow the actual instructions rather than copying what another household has left at the kerb.

Another mistake is assuming that a fee covers every possible material or task. A normal furniture collection is not automatically a specialist waste service. Separate unknown liquids, chemicals or other unusual items and ask for the appropriate route instead of concealing them inside drawers or boxes. If you discover something after the quote is agreed, report it. A revised plan before the visit is more useful than a surprise when the team is already at the door.

## Your checklist

- Decide the intended route first.
- Check current local instructions.
- Keep useful furniture protected.
- Disclose defects.
- Do not leave items in public space without authorisation.
