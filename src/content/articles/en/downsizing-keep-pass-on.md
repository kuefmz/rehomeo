Choose what fits the next home before deciding how to clear the old one. Measure rooms, doorways and furniture, then prioritise pieces you use and can place comfortably. Keeping everything “just in case” may postpone the decision and make storage part of the problem.

## Plan the details

Work by function: sleeping, eating, seating and storage. Compare duplicate items and choose the piece that suits your actual needs. Set aside sentimental belongings for a deliberate decision rather than letting them disappear into collection boxes. A staged inventory helps relatives or housemates agree without rushing at the doorway.

## A practical example

If your next home has space for one table, compare the tables by size, function and condition before pricing storage. The smaller one may suit daily needs better even if the larger one was expensive. Decide deliberately, then place the table to pass on in the inventory with its dimensions and known defects.

A useful test is to draw a rough layout of the next home and place the main furniture on it using measurements. This need not be a design exercise: it simply reveals duplicates and pieces that cannot fit their intended role. Review the list again after a short pause before agreeing collection. If someone else owns a shared item, record their decision separately. Downsizing should simplify the next home without turning a rushed choice into an irreversible loss.

## Your checklist

- Measure the next home.
- Choose by everyday function.
- Review duplicate pieces.
- Separate sentimental belongings.
- Make an agreed pass-on list.
