A personalised quote depends on the work, not just the number of rooms. Item volume, weight, floor, lift access, carry distance, dismantling, travel and agreed handling can change the scope. A small apartment with awkward access can require different work from a larger, easily accessible one.

## Plan the details

Give enough information to make the quote meaningful: an inventory, dimensions for large pieces, known defects and access details. Ask what is included, what would trigger a revised price and how changes are approved. Rehomeo does not publish invented fixed prices or assume that possible resale or donation pays for the collection.

## A practical example

Two collections of five items can involve different work. One may have ground-floor access and intact furniture; the other may require stairs, a long carry and dismantling. Compare the included tasks, not just the headline price. If you add items after receiving the quote, seek an updated agreement before collection.

Compare quotes against the same inventory and access description. Ask whether the quoted total includes the agreed handling, travel and dismantling, and whether any charges depend on what is found on site. Do not assume one provider has omitted a charge just because their wording is shorter. If an offer is unclear, ask for clarification before accepting. Keep a copy of the agreed scope so changes in quantity or access can be discussed against a common starting point.

## Your checklist

- Describe volume and access.
- List dismantling needs.
- Clarify agreed handling routes.
- Ask about exclusions and changes.
- Approve scope price and date separately.
