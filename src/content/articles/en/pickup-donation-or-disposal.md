Pickup describes the logistics of removing an item. Donation describes a transfer without a sale. Disposal describes an end-of-use route. These terms are not interchangeable: paying for collection does not determine where the furniture goes, and offering an item free does not guarantee a recipient.

## Plan the details

For furniture that is safe and useful, first explore a realistic reuse route. Ask potential recipients about their needs and practical acceptance conditions rather than arriving unannounced. For damaged or unsuitable items, clarify the agreed handling and check the relevant local authority’s current instructions. Rehomeo assesses requests individually and cannot guarantee a donation.

## A practical example

If you have a usable bookcase but no way to transport it, a free offer and a collection service answer different questions. Confirm whether a recipient wants the bookcase, then arrange the transport and its cost. If no recipient is available, ask about alternatives rather than treating the initial offer as a completed donation.

Choose a route using four questions: is the item safe, is there a recipient, can the transfer happen on time, and who bears the transport cost? If one answer is missing, it remains a plan rather than an outcome. You can ask for collection while still clarifying suitability, but the agreement should identify how that uncertainty will be handled. Do not leave the provider to discover different expectations only after loading has started.

## Your checklist

- Assess safety and usefulness.
- Check recipient needs before arranging transport.
- Separate collection fees from item value.
- Agree any resale route.
- Confirm local disposal instructions where needed.
